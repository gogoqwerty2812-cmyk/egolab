Protein is the raw material for new muscle, but more is not always better. The research gives a fairly clear range — here is how to work out your own number and hit it without overthinking.

The number: about 1.6 g per kg

A large 2018 meta-analysis of resistance-training studies found that the benefit of extra protein for muscle gain levels off at around 1.6 g per kg of body weight per day. Some people kept improving slightly up to about 2.2 g/kg, so that is a reasonable upper end.

If you carry a lot of body fat, base the calculation on your goal weight rather than your current weight.

When to aim higher

  • Dieting to lose fat: a higher intake (towards 2.2 g/kg) helps protect muscle while you eat less.
  • Older lifters: muscles respond less to each meal with age, so slightly larger protein servings help.
  • Very high training volume: the upper half of the range is a safe bet.

How to split it across the day

You do not need to measure every meal, but spreading protein out works better than one huge dinner. A simple target is 3–5 meals with 20–40 g of protein each (about 0.3–0.4 g per kg per meal). The popular "your body can only use 30 g at once" rule is a myth — larger servings are still used, just more slowly. Your daily total is what counts most.

Timing around training matters less than people think. If you eat a protein-rich meal within a few hours before or after a workout, you are covered.

Food first, powder second

Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, tofu, tempeh and legumes all count. Protein powder is simply a convenient, cheap source when meals fall short — for example a shake after training or with breakfast. Is it safe?

For healthy adults, protein intakes in this range have not been shown to damage the kidneys or bones. Drink enough water and keep the rest of your diet varied. If you have kidney disease, follow your doctor's guidance rather than these numbers.

The short version

  • Aim for 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight per day; up to 2.2 g/kg when dieting.
  • Spread it over 3–5 meals of 20–40 g.
  • Total intake matters more than timing.
  • Protein powder is a convenience, not a must.
