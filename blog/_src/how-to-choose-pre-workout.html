Most pre-workouts are built around the same few ingredients. The difference between a good one and a bad one is mostly whether those ingredients are dosed properly — and whether the label tells you the truth. Here is how to read one.

The ingredients that do the work

IngredientEffective doseWhat it does
Caffeineabout 3 mg per kg (150–300 mg)Energy, focus, slightly more strength and endurance
L-citrulline6–8 g (or 8 g citrulline malate)Blood flow, "pump", may reduce fatigue
Beta-alanine3.2–6.4 g per dayHelps in hard efforts of 1–4 minutes; works with daily use
Creatine3–5 g per dayStrength and power; works with daily use, not per dose

Many products add tyrosine, taurine, betaine, electrolytes or nootropics. Some of these have modest evidence; none matter as much as the four above. If a label lists citrulline at 1 g or beta-alanine at 1 g, it is decoration, not a dose.

Caffeine: know your total

The European Food Safety Authority considers up to 200 mg in one go and up to 400 mg per day safe for healthy adults (less if you are pregnant). A single scoop of pre-workout often contains 200–350 mg. Add a morning coffee (around 80–100 mg) and an energy drink, and it is easy to go far past the daily limit.

  • New to stimulants? Start with half a scoop and see how you react.
  • Training in the evening? Caffeine lingers for hours. Within 6–8 hours of bed, pick a stimulant-free ("stim-free") formula.
  • Heart condition, high blood pressure, anxiety, or on medication? Ask a doctor before using stimulants.

The tingle and the crash

The prickly, tingling feeling on your face and hands comes from beta-alanine. It is harmless and wears off. If you dislike it, choose a product with less beta-alanine or take your daily beta-alanine split into smaller doses with meals.

A "crash" an hour or two after training is usually just too much caffeine. Lower the dose, and do not use pre-workout to make up for poor sleep.

Label red flags

  • Proprietary blends — one total weight for a "matrix" of ingredients, with no individual amounts.
  • DMAA, DMHA, DMBA and similar stimulants — unapproved and linked to serious side effects. Avoid them.
  • Vague stimulant names you cannot look up, or caffeine content not stated in milligrams.
  • No batch lab report — for anything with stimulants, third-party testing for contaminants and banned substances is worth having. See how to read a lab report.

How to choose, quickly

  1. Decide on stimulant or stim-free based on when you train.
  2. Check caffeine in mg and make sure it fits your daily total.
  3. Look for citrulline at 6 g or more and beta-alanine at 3 g or more.
  4. Skip proprietary blends and anything with banned stimulants.
  5. Prefer products with batch lab reports.
