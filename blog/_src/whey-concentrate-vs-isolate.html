All whey comes from the same place — the liquid left over when milk is turned into cheese. The difference between concentrate, isolate and hydrolysate is simply how far it has been filtered. Here is how that changes what you get, and how to choose.

Whey concentrate

Concentrate is filtered the least. It keeps a little fat and lactose, which is why it tastes creamier and mixes into a thicker shake. It is the best value per gram of protein. If dairy does not bother you, concentrate is a perfectly good choice for muscle building — most research on whey and muscle growth used ordinary whey.

Whey isolate

Isolate goes through extra filtration that removes most of the fat and lactose, leaving 90% protein or more. You get a few more grams of protein per scoop and fewer calories from anything else. The main reason to pay more for it is your stomach: if regular whey leaves you bloated, an isolate often solves that. It is also handy when you are counting every calorie during a diet.

Whey hydrolysate

Hydrolysate is whey that has been partly broken down so it digests even faster. It is useful in some medical nutrition and for people with very sensitive digestion, but for gym-goers the advantage over isolate is small and the price is not. Unless you have a specific reason, you can skip it.

How to choose

  • No issues with dairy, want the best value: concentrate.
  • Lactose-sensitive, or cutting and counting calories: isolate.
  • Want to mix with milk or oats anyway: concentrate — the lactose difference disappears in a milk shake.
  • Dairy-free: whey is not for you; look at pea, soy or rice protein blends.

Reading the label

Compare protein per 100 g, not per scoop — brands use different scoop sizes to make numbers look better. A quality concentrate shows around 75–80 g per 100 g; an isolate around 85–90 g.

Check the full amino acid profile. Cheap amino acids like glycine or taurine are sometimes added to inflate the protein figure ("amino spiking"). A third-party lab report that measures actual protein content is the easiest way to know you are getting what is on the tub. Our guide explains what to look for in a lab report.

How much and when

A scoop is a convenient way to top up protein, not a requirement. What counts is your total for the day — see how much protein you need to build muscle. One or two scoops a day, whenever it is convenient, is a sensible use for most people.
